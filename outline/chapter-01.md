## Purpose

Chapter 1 should:

1. make the reader care about one immediate person before asking them to care about the entire world;
2. establish that this is a fantasy world through practical lived detail;
3. introduce a local conflict with a complete beginning, escalation, decision, and resolution;
4. show that characters have pre-existing relationships and responsibilities;
5. establish one or more world elements that can recur naturally;
6. create at least two continuity threads for later stories;
7. end with a meaningful change, not a teaser pretending to be an ending.

The chapter should **not**:
- explain the full magic system;
- introduce the entire recurring cast;
- list world history;
- announce the book's overarching plot;
- include arbitrary cameos solely so readers remember names later.

## Escalation

Use consequences rather than random additional problems.

A useful escalation chain:

1. **Problem appears.**
2. Protagonist takes the obvious action.
3. The action partly works but creates a cost.
4. New information changes their understanding.
5. Their available choices narrow.
6. They make a decision that cannot be cleanly reversed.
7. Consequences produce the climax.
8. The ending establishes a new state.

## Scenes

The chapter should probably contain 7-12 substantial scenes rather than dozens of tiny beats.

### Scene 1 — Ordinary life under pressure

**Purpose**
- Introduce the POV character in motion.
- Establish one relationship or responsibility.
- Show fantasy-world normality through practical details.
- Reveal an existing small pressure before the main disturbance.

**Scene question**
> Can the protagonist get through this ordinary problem without making their life worse?

**Exit condition**
Something changes or arrives that makes the larger story unavoidable.

---

### Scene 2 — The disturbance

**Purpose**
- Deliver the inciting event.
- Make its stakes personal.
- Let the protagonist form an initial plan.

Avoid stopping for a worldbuilding lecture.

---

### Scene 3 — First attempt

**Purpose**
- The protagonist acts.
- The reader sees competence and limitation.
- At least one secondary character has their own agenda.

**Result**
Partial success, failure, or success with an unexpected cost.

---

### Scene 4 — Social consequence

**Purpose**
Show that the problem exists inside a human network.

This is a good place for:
- family disagreement;
- workplace pressure;
- romantic friction;
- debt;
- political disagreement;
- reputation;
- old history resurfacing.

Do not make every supporting character agree with the protagonist.

---

### Scene 5 — New information

The protagonist learns something that changes what the story means.

It should not merely increase danger.

Examples:
- the supposed victim made a choice;
- the law is being enforced for a reason the protagonist did not know;
- an ally has conflicting obligations;
- the missing object was deliberately abandoned;
- the magical effect has a different cause;
- the person asking for help has lied.

---

### Scene 6 — Narrowing options

The protagonist now has fewer comfortable choices.

They should need to decide what they are willing to sacrifice.

---

### Scene 7 — Irreversible choice

This is the decision the ending will judge.

The protagonist takes responsibility for an action.

Prefer:
> They choose.

Over:
> Something happens to them.

---

### Scene 8 — Climax / immediate consequence

The consequences of the choice arrive.

The climax can be:
- confrontation;
- escape;
- negotiation;
- confession;
- ritual;
- betrayal;
- public hearing;
- theft;
- rescue;
- quiet personal refusal.

Scale should match the story.

---

### Scene 9 — Aftermath

Show the new reality.

Include enough ordinary detail to make the consequence tangible.

Questions:
- What has been gained?
- What has been lost?
- Who now sees the protagonist differently?
- What practical problem remains tomorrow morning?

---

### Scene 10 — Closing image / hand-off

End the local story.

Optionally include a small element that later stories can pick up:
- a letter sent;
- a name entered into a ledger;
- an object changing hands;
- a road closing;
- a rumour starting;
- someone leaving town;
- a job vacancy;
- a decree posted.

This should feel like part of the protagonist's ending, not an advertisement for Chapter 2.

## Example only — worked Story 1 skeleton

> **NON-CANONICAL EXAMPLE.**
> This exists to demonstrate the amount of detail expected from an outline before prose generation.

### Premise

A municipal apprentice responsible for maintaining legal ward-marks discovers that a poor residential building has been using an illegal protective charm.

Reporting it will make the building unsafe until the occupants can pay for sanctioned work.

Ignoring it risks his licence and could make him personally liable if the charm fails.

### Starting state

The apprentice:
- is technically competent;
- desperately needs his licence renewed;
- financially supports a younger sibling;
- believes rules are annoying but generally exist for good reasons;
- has no appetite for becoming a hero;
- knows the building's caretaker casually from his daily route.

### Disturbance

During a scheduled inspection, he discovers that the illegal ward was built from fragments of old military magic.

It is both prohibited and currently the only reason the building remains habitable.

### First plan

He gives the caretaker three days to replace it before filing the report.

He believes this is a generous compromise.

### Complication

The caretaker cannot afford replacement.

Worse, the legal ward contractor is owned by the apprentice's employer's brother.

The "safety rule" is partly legitimate and partly a monopoly.

### New information

The apprentice learns the illegal ward was installed by a former soldier living in the building, who understands its risks better than he expected.

The ward is unsafe, but not in the way the regulation claims.

### Irreversible choice

He falsifies the inspection date to buy the building time.

This protects the residents but creates documentary evidence that can later be traced to him.

### Local ending

The residents obtain a safer temporary solution.

The apprentice keeps his job.

He has not solved institutional corruption.

He has crossed a line he previously believed he would never cross.

### Threads carried forward

- the caretaker may reappear as a supporting character;
- the former soldier may later become a POV character;
- the contractor can affect another story without the apprentice appearing;
- the falsified inspection can become evidence in a later investigation;
- residents begin telling an exaggerated version of what the apprentice did;
- a later character may know him only through that inaccurate story.

The first story is complete, but the world has retained its consequences.
