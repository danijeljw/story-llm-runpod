## Core concept

Marek Ilyan is the man people initially assume must be the quieter half of the relationship.

He is not.

He is simply better at choosing when to be visible.

Where Luca is recognisable because he is everywhere, Marek is memorable because he makes people feel that something is happening just beneath the surface.

He is tall, dark-featured, physically striking and self-possessed. He is conventionally white, but has dark hair, dark eyes, olive-to-tan skin and sharper features that give him a more severe, less sunlit beauty than Luca.

If Luca looks like summer, Marek looks like midnight in an expensive hotel bar.

He is friendly.

He smiles.

He can be charming.

He knows exactly how attractive he is.

He also has a much more private appetite for control, intensity, risk and sexual experimentation than most people in Luca's public-facing world realise.

Marek is not socially cruel, unstable or chaotic.

He is, however, much less interested than Luca in being universally liked.

He does not need everyone to approve of him.

That gives him a freedom Luca sometimes envies.

Marek's most useful contradiction is that he is highly disciplined in work and highly permissive with himself in pleasure.

He can spend twelve hours reviewing a regulatory case involving difficult ethical detail, then disappear into a club until sunrise, sleep three hours, shower, dress perfectly and walk into a breakfast meeting as though the night never happened.

That capacity is impressive.

It is also not sustainable forever.

## Arc

### Book-level starting state

Marek begins as a man who believes his life works because he understands the rules.

He understands:

- law;
- medicine;
- institutions;
- consent;
- relationships;
- risk;
- social reputation;
- sexual boundaries.

He is usually correct.

His problem is that he mistakes understanding for immunity.

### Important turning points

Possible turning points:

1. A casual partner becomes emotionally attached despite clear boundaries.
2. A nightlife acquaintance appears unexpectedly in a professional context.
3. Marek is forced to acknowledge he participated in a situation while more impaired than he considers acceptable.
4. Luca asks a question Marek cannot answer with technical honesty alone.
5. A professional case mirrors something Marek privately rationalises.
6. Someone less experienced treats Marek as an authority in a risky environment.
7. Marek realises he has been using "I can handle it" as a substitute for deciding whether something is good for him.
8. He has to choose between professional reputation and protecting someone vulnerable.
9. Luca stops balancing him automatically and makes Marek define his own limit.

### Current trajectory

From:

> "I know exactly what I'm doing."

Toward:

> "Knowing what I'm doing does not guarantee I should keep doing it."

### Possible future direction

Marek should not be morally "fixed" into conventionality.

Growth does not require:

- monogamy;
- celibacy;
- abandoning kink;
- abandoning clubs;
- becoming sexually conservative.

Instead, possible growth involves:

- stronger self-awareness;
- more honest risk assessment;
- less reliance on compartmentalisation;
- accepting vulnerability;
- distinguishing freedom from compulsion;
- becoming better at recognising when another person's consent is complicated by status, intoxication or emotional dependency;
- allowing Luca to see him before he has solved the problem.

Show dominance through behaviour rather than endless labels.

Examples:

- waits for explicit agreement;
- gives a simple instruction and watches whether it is followed;
- notices hesitation immediately;
- stops when consent changes;
- controls pace without unnecessary aggression;
- uses quiet rather than volume;
- checks in without breaking character.

In professional scenes:

In nightlife:

- looser;
- more physical;
- more amused;
- more openly sexual.

In private with Luca:

### "Love to hate" quality

Marek should sometimes irritate readers because:

- he gets away with things;
- he is too attractive;
- he is too confident;
- he knows exactly what he can get away with;
- he can be emotionally detached;

- he does not pretend to be morally purer than he is;
- he respects consent;
- he protects confidentiality;
- he genuinely loves Luca;
- he often behaves better than people who publicly judge him.

The correct feeling is generally **love to hate**, with occasional drift into **hate to love** depending on POV.
