# Timeline

This file tracks canonical chronology across all stories.

Because stories may overlap, skip time, revisit events, or follow different characters, the timeline is authoritative even when publication order is not chronological.

---

## Dating system

Choose one canonical system before drafting heavily.

Possible options:

- exact in-world calendar;
- year / season / day;
- relative dates anchored to a known event;
- hybrid.

Recommended internal format even if dates never appear in prose:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD
```

or:

```text
Year 412, Autumn, Day 18
```

Consistency matters more than what system is chosen.

## Example only — overlapping chronology

> **NON-CANONICAL EXAMPLE.**

| Date | Event | Story relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Autumn 03 | Mara falsifies an inspection entry | Seen directly in Story 1 |
| Autumn 05 | Registry begins unrelated permit audit | Mentioned as background in Story 1 |
| Autumn 08 | Tomas delivers audit notices | Opening event of Story 2 |
| Autumn 09 | Mara hears that "some registry problem" is delaying licences | Minor background consequence |
| Autumn 12 | Investigator receives evidence of several forged documents | Story 3 begins |
| Autumn 14 | A tavern rumour falsely links the forged documents to the ward scandal | Heard by Story 4 POV |
| Autumn 18 | Mara learns an investigator visited her former supervisor | Occurs off-page; relevant later |

The important distinction:

**event order** and **story order** do not have to be identical.

A later story can reveal that something important was already happening during an earlier story.

## Canon-lock policy

Use status markers:

- `PLANNED` — outline only; may change freely.
- `DRAFTED` — appears in prose but can still be revised.
- `LOCKED` — published or explicitly declared canonical.

When changing a `LOCKED` event:
1. identify every dependent character, relationship, and story;
2. update them deliberately;
3. never silently retcon the timeline.
